Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common client inquiries regarding glass types, safety standards, customization tolerances, and ordering procedures in Jodhpur.

Toughened (or tempered) glass is produced by heating standard annealed float glass to approximately 650°C and then rapidly cooling its surfaces with high-pressure air streams. This process creates high compressive stress on the exterior surfaces and tensile stress within the core, making it 4 to 5 times stronger than regular glass. If broken, it shatters into small, relatively harmless blunt dice rather than jagged, dangerous shards.
No. Once glass has undergone the heat-tempering process, it cannot be cut, drilled, grooved, or edge-trimmed. Any attempt to cut or drill toughened glass will instantly trigger complete shattering due to balanced internal stresses. Therefore, all exact dimensions, corner notches, hinge cutouts, and hole locations must be precision machined prior to tempering.
Depending on the application, we supply glass typically ranging from 4mm to 19mm. For example:
  • 4mm – 6mm: Cabinet shutters, small windows, standard wall mirrors.
  • 8mm – 10mm: Shower enclosures, interior slim partitions, frameless sliding systems.
  • 12mm: Heavy commercial doors, frameless storefronts, staircase balustrades.
  • 15mm – 19mm / Laminated: High-load structural glass railings, glass canopies, and floors.

For initial estimating, rough measurements using a tape measure (Width × Height in millimeters, inches, or feet) are sufficient. For final fabrication, dimensions should always be taken at 3 points (top, middle, bottom for width; left, center, right for height) after civil and tile work is completely finished to account for any out-of-plumb walls.
